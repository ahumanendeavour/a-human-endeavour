  • Banjo and Ro's Grand Island Hotel
    After watching every series of Interior Design Masters for house ideas we noticed some of the contestants ended up with their own spin-off shows, the most interesting of which seemed to be this one. It follows one of the winners and his partner renovating a ruined manor house on an outer Hebridean island in Scotland, trying to get it ready for the winter.     📖
  • The Witcher - Blood of Elves - Andrzej Sapkowski
    This was an audiobook I've been keen to get around to, partly because I wanted to listen to the book anyway, but also because I'm always interested to see how narrators tackle audiobooks with so many characters. Steven Fry's narration of the Harry Potter books relies a lot more on tone of voice than it does on accents (though there are a few great ones in there), whereas this was a very accent-heavy read. Peter Kenny has done a terrific job keeping so many characters distinct across such a wordy novel. There's never any doubt as to who's talking, and even one-line incidental characters are given the respect they deserve.
  • Cleopatra and Frankenstien by Coco Mellors
    We finally finished it last night. What a ride. The ending was gorgeous, though it did leave me wanting an epilogue or something, just to see where the characters ended up. Rather than leaving anyone with a solid conclusion, we leave them somewhere in transition, starting new phases of their lives. I suppose that can only be a good thing; lets them live on in the imagination.

  • The Pitt
    We finished the second season of The Pitt and loved it. It's probably my favourite show of the past ten years. Yes, I had my doubts about the second season from the first couple of episodes, but the rest of the season absolutely saved it.

  • Shrinking
    I'm really in two minds about Shrinking. On the one hand some of the jokes land perfectly, and the acting is stellar throughout (especially from Harrison Ford); but the dialogue is really starting to bug me. We're about half way through the first season and so far it seems like every conversation the characters have with each other are the same. They're all the same level of friends with eachother, so anyone can have a heart-to-heart with anyone, making it feel like character relationships don't matter, no matter how much the show wants you to believe otherwise. I'm enjoying it, but I'm not sure if I'm enjoying it enough for three seasons of it.

  • The Night Manager
    We watched Season 2 of Night Manager and both of us really enjoyed it, which was a surprise. It was interesting seeing the ending line up a little more with the original novel tonally, and the tee-up for a third season was a nice surprise. I do think the show suffered a little in the writing department, specifically with the dialogue. LeCarre dialogue has a flavour to it, a gentle, almost delicate formality that barely covers a bloodied-fist brutality. The new show at times felt like a generic spy show.

  • Slow Horses
    I showed my partner Slow Horses almost as a thank you for indulging me with Night Manager. She enjoys Slow Horses a lot more, which is understandable: Horses is probably a better show disregarding my own preferences. Originally I watched season 1 and 2 in quick succession, but found the character Roddy Ho to be too unbearable to continue (that's not a criticism by the way, I think the character is brilliantly performed and written, he just pushes my own buttons a little too well.). I took a break from it for a while before coming back to season 3 myself, and I'm glad I did. It's fantastic from top to bottom.
    📖
  • There is no Antimemetics Division by QNTM
    I've been a fan of the SCP fictional universe for a long time, well over a decade. I remember reading their stories at my first job when I really should have been doing other things. This novelisation of one of the sites most popular stories caught my attention and piqued my curiosity. I'm not completely done with it yet, but so far it's a word for word copy of the original with references to the SCP foundation changed to a "legally distinct" organisation that allows the book to function as a standalone piece. I definitely think the target audience for this book is not established SCP fans, as the changes come across a little clunky. Thankfully the book still captures the thrills of the original, and new readers can still engage with the concepts without feeling like they've crashed a party they weren't invited to.

  • Titus Andronicus - by William Shakespeare
  • True and False - by David Mammet

    • Decided to revisit True and False since I was getting more optimistic about my acting. Of all the books to read before starting drama school, True and False is probably the worst, but that's when I read it. Whilst it's full of useful information and relatable experiences, it does come across very anti-institutional when it comes to training. The older and more experienced I get, the more intimately I relate to the feelings he describes. The feeling of inadequacy when techniques bolstered by prestigue don't feel like they're connecting with you. The mental prisons we put outselves in when we convince ourselves that if we don't do x amount of physical and vocal exercises we will be completely incapable of any kind of emotional connection. Whilst I agree with some of it, I can't deny my own experiences of improvement, connection, and inspiration that come from the aspects of the work he dismisses. Like everything, creating your own system is all about picking and choosing what works for you, and I am reminded of that every time I read this book. Big recommend, especially for young actors.

    Titus Andronicus is a play I haven't read in well over ten years, and havent seen in 15. It's famous for being one of his bloodier plays, and it deserves its reputation. There's a wonderful clip of Brian Cox playing the title role in a masterclass that I come back to every so often as there's so much to learn from it. I remember watching it a lot when I was in my teens auditioning for drama schools and practicing my monologues. I'm really enjoying reading Shakespeare again. I think it's reminding me of the work I love, and what I love about the work. I set myself the goal of learning two new classical and contemporary speeches before the end of the year, like I used to every year for audition season, and I've found it really exciting. Maybe worth a post.
